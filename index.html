    How Reclaim works

    Zankalon screening (default)

    1. The map — 1,780 squares of 100 m over the Zankalon pilot rectangle, coloured by what happened to greenness between 2023 and 2024. Orange = visit first.
    2. Click a square — its status, the plain-language reason, the median change of four signals, and month-by-month charts for both years. Grey bands are months left out because clouds broke the quality rules.
    3. Visit-first shortlist — the 98 orange squares, most negative first. Click a row to fly there.
    4. Data quality and Data sources — how many cells passed each month, what changes at other thresholds, and where every number comes from.

    Everything shown is read from the notebook output files (results/); the app recomputes nothing. "Explore other fields" switches to the original Tanager field-check tools for other places.

    Explore other fields

    1. My field — pick an example, or zoom in and click the corners of your field. Pixels touching the outline are left out, because they mix your field with the land next to it.
    2. Check my field — Reclaim finds every satellite image that covers your field and uses them all.
    3. Field health — overall status, what to check first, and four health checks.
    4. Change over time — is the field declining season after season while the land around it is not?

    Satellite images

    Each image is one satellite pass over your field. 1 image shows today's condition. 2 images add the change between dates. 3+ images across years add the long-term land-degradation check. Real images and made-up demo images are never mixed in one check.

    What each number of images gives you

    ImagesYou get
    1Today's condition, weak patches, comparison with the land around the field
    2+ change between the two dates, whether weak patches are spreading
    3+ over several years+ trend, comparison with earlier seasons, land-degradation check, which signal warned first

    Anything that needs more images says Needs more images and explains why — Reclaim never guesses.

    Moving around the globe

    • Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag or Ctrl-drag to tilt.
    • Round buttons at the top: find my location, tilt, north up, zoom to my field, whole Earth — then copy link, hide panels, help.
    • Drag any panel by its title bar to move it around the screen; click the title to fold it into a pill. “Display → Put panels back in place” resets them.
    • Search: type a country or place and pick a suggestion, or press Enter to go to the first one.
    • Click a green pin or a highlighted image outline to fly there.
    • While drawing: left click adds a corner, right click removes the last one, click the first corner to finish, Esc cancels.

    Location: “Find my location” asks your browser first; if you say no, it uses an approximate city-level position from your internet address (GeoJS). Place search uses OpenStreetMap data (Photon / Nominatim). Nothing is stored.
